# CITATIONS-by-state.md — Manifest of Every Number Used in the State-Level Tool

Companion to `files/CITATIONS.md` (the original RTO/utility-territory tool's manifest). This file
covers `data-center-tax-tool-by-state.html` specifically — a separate tool, separate `CITATIONS`
array, no shared rows with the original.

Every dollar figure, rate, percentage, or forecast displayed anywhere in the state-level tool must
have a row here. If a number appears in the tool but has no row here, that is a bug — see
`files/TESTING-by-state.md`.

Ask Claude Code to update this file automatically every time a new hardcoded number is added to
`data-center-tax-tool-by-state.html`, rather than maintaining it by hand separately from the code.

## Format

| Value Used | What It Represents | Region | Source Document | Exact Quote or Location | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Value Used** — the literal number as it appears in the code/tool.
- **What It Represents** — plain-language description.
- **Region** — which state this applies to, or "All" if it's a universal constant.
- **Source Document** — the specific file this came from.
- **Exact Quote or Location** — the specific sentence, table, or column the number came from, so it can be re-checked without re-reading the whole document.
- **Confidence** — one of: **Sourced** (directly stated in the source), **Derived** (calculated from sourced figures, show the calculation), **Illustrative** (an assumption or a third-party estimate, not a published forecast — must be visibly flagged in the tool itself, not just here), **NEEDS SOURCE** (currently unsourced — should not be in the live tool until resolved).

## What's different from the region-level manifest

Every household, burden, price, bill, and buildout row here is a **direct read** of a state-native
source — DOE LEAD's own state totals, EIA-861's own State column, EPA eGRID's own ST23 sheet,
dcmap.us's own state aggregates. None of these rows involve the EIA-861-customer-share
apportionment the region-level manifest depends on for every regional figure, so there is no
`exclusions.py`/`REASSIGNMENTS` mechanism and no wrong-territory risk to document here — see
`sources/17-state-level/state-pilot.py` for the single script behind every row below.

## What each source does in the model

Generated from the `SOURCE_GROUPS` array in the tool, which also drives its Sources section.
Citations that differ only by jurisdiction share one entry: the sentence is the same for all
of them, and the count in brackets says how many rows it covers.

### The fee itself

_The rate charged per kilowatt-hour, and how it changes over time._

- **`rate-low`** — Sets the two rates under the Low tier. Fossil-counted kWh pay the first, clean-counted kWh the second.
- **`rate-mod`** — Sets the two rates under the Moderate tier, the tool's default.
- **`rate-agg`** — Sets the two rates under the Aggressive tier.
- **`rate-ratio`** — Sets how much more a fossil kilowatt-hour pays than a clean one. At 1 the two are equal and the fee is flat.
- **`ratio`** — Fixes two ratios the tool never varies: fossil is charged exactly twice the clean rate, and a severely burdened household receives exactly twice a moderately burdened one.
- **`rate-flat`** — Under the Flat structure, replaces the two rates with one. It is the selected tier's two rates blended at this territory's measured grid mix, so flat and differentiated raise identical revenue at enactment.
- **`rate-flat-options`** — The fixed alternatives to that blend, selectable instead of it. Choosing one makes the tier buttons inert, and the tool grays them out.
- **`grid-indexing`** — Multiplies the rate as the territory's fossil share falls, so revenue does not shrink simply because the grid cleaned. Held steady, revenue is constant whatever the grid burns; at the left end the index is off and a cleaner grid shrinks the dividend.
- **`cpi`** — Grows the rate each year after enactment when CPI indexing is on. Applies to the rate on paper, not to the dividend.
- **`va-enacted`** — The benchmark all three fee tiers are set against: an enacted US data center electricity tax, used so the tiers are anchored to a real statute rather than chosen freely.

### How much electricity is charged

_Converting a data center buildout into billable kilowatt-hours._

- **`*-buildout`** _(51 rows)_ — The megawatts charged. This is the first term of the revenue formula and the slider at the top of Section 2. High = every lifecycle status including paused/inactive — a ceiling, not a forecast.
- **`dc-definition`** — What the word “data center” covers in the buildout above — the tool inherits the definition rather than setting one. Enters no calculation; it says what the megawatts are counting.
- **`loadfactor`** — The second term. Megawatts are multiplied by this share to get average draw.
- **`lbnl-load-factor`** — A sourced alternative to the dial above, shown beside it so the reader can see what a published figure would give instead.
- **`eligibility`** — Sets the divisor: who the revenue is shared among. All three settings raise identical revenue, so this changes who is paid and how much each receives, never how much is collected.
- **`lbnl-flexibility-gap`** — A recorded absence, from the same report. Load shifting is the likeliest way a facility could owe less without consuming less; LBNL states the research to quantify it has not been done, so the tool models no such effect.
- **`lbnl-elasticity-gap`** — A recorded absence, from the same report. LBNL states the link between the price of cloud and AI services and their power demand “is not well understood”, which is why the price-sensitivity dial defaults to no response.
- **`hours`** — The third term: hours in a year. Converts average megawatts into megawatt-hours.
- **`kwpermw`** — Converts megawatts to kilowatts — the first conversion in the formula above — so a per-kWh rate can be applied. The same entry documents megawatts per gigawatt, which is how the buildout slider is read.
- **`slider-range`** — The minimum, maximum and step of the buildout slider for this territory, and the values behind its scenario buttons.
- **`elasticity`** — Shrinks the buildout in response to the fee before revenue is computed. Defaults to zero — no response.
- **`lbnl-retail-context`** — Context for what the tool does not model: the effect of data center load on the underlying electricity rate. Enters no calculation.

### What counts as fossil

_How each kilowatt-hour is classified, which decides the rate it pays._

- **`egrid-fossil`** — The territory's measured fossil share of generation. It is the default position of the fossil-share slider, and the reference the grid index is normalized against.
- **`split`** — The share of charged load counted as fossil rather than clean. A user-set dial; the tool's own measured grid mix is offered as the default.
- **`fossil-design`** — The rule that decides which rate a kilowatt-hour pays, including that hourly granular certificates qualify for the clean rate and unbundled annual RECs do not.
- **`hourly-practice`** — Evidence that hour-by-hour matching is an operating measurement today, not only a proposal. Supports the design being feasible; enters no calculation.
- **`granular-cert-accreditation`** — The maturity of the certificate regime the clean rate would rely on. Enters no calculation.
- **`btm-gas-pipeline`** — The gas capacity being built for data centers, split into on-site and grid-connected. The source for the on-site shares below.
- **`btm-gas-share`** — The share of this territory's data center load served by on-site gas. Applied as a floor: that share is fossil-rated whatever the facility procures, and the fossil-share slider only governs the rest.
- **`btm-mode`** — Which bound of that share the tool applies. Published as a low and a high bound with no point estimate, because the source leaves the plant's status unrecorded for part of the capacity.
- **`btm-renewables-gap`** — A recorded absence. No source was found that quantifies on-site renewable capacity at US data centers, so the tool applies no renewable equivalent to the gas floor.

### Who receives the dividend

_The households the money is divided among._

- **`*-hh`** _(51 rows)_ — The denominator. Revenue is divided among the burdened households within this count.
- **`burden-*`** _(51 rows)_ — Multiplied by the household count to give how many households qualify at each tier. Measured, not set — it is why the same fee produces different dividends in different places.
- **`thresholds`** — Defines the two tiers those shares measure: above 10% of income on energy is severe, 6–10% is moderate.
- **`energy-insecurity`** — What the thresholds correspond to in behavior, nationally. Context for reading the shares; enters no calculation.
- **`insecurity-by-income`** — The income gradient of that same national measure. Enters no calculation.
- **`burden-simplification`** — How the dividend is applied to a household's energy burden when the tool reports the burden after payment.
- **`burden-vs-average`** — Why this tool’s burden figure is far above the one LEAD shows on its own site: LEAD reports the typical household’s burden, this reports the share of households above a threshold, and the distribution is long-tailed. Enters no calculation — it reconciles two numbers a reader will otherwise think contradict each other.
- **`burden-method`** — The known limits of those measured shares — within-cell averaging flattens both tails, households reporting no income are excluded, and the resulting share is then applied to all households. Every one of them pushes the severe count the same way: down.
- **`burden-profile`** — The per-tier energy and income figures behind the burden calculation: what each tier spends on home energy, what it earns, and so what a dividend does to its burden.
- **`takeup`** — The share of qualifying households assumed to actually receive the money. Scales the payout up per household as it falls, since the same revenue is split fewer ways.
- **`takeup-redistribution`** — Fixes what happens to money that eligible households do not claim: it is not shared out among those who do, so the per-household payment does not move with take-up.
- **`months`** — Divides the annual dividend into the monthly figure the tool reports.
- **`delivery`** — How the payment reaches the household — bill credit first, then a check for any remainder.

### What households and data centers pay today

_The prices the fee is added to and compared against._

- **`industrial-price`** — The price the fee is added to when the tool reports what the charge costs the industry. An average across all large customers, not a data center tariff.
- **`residential-price`** — Compared against the large-customer price in the baseline card, to state what a household pays per kilowatt-hour against what a large customer pays.
- **`bill-*`** _(51 rows)_ — The bill the dividend is measured against, and the basis for splitting it into the share of a bill it covers. Direct state-level computation, no BA-code or utility-name crosswalk. An all-customer average, not a burdened-household bill specifically.
- **`bill-dist`** — The distribution behind that average — households per $10 bill bucket, by tier. It is what lets the tool say how many households the dividend fully covers, rather than only how it compares with a mean.
- **`seasonality`** — Spreads that average bill across the months of the year, so the tool can report how many months the dividend would cover rather than only an annual average.

### The national floor, and what the dividend is measured against

_Revenue set aside before local distribution, and the existing programs the payout is compared with._

- **`sb253`** — A reference line beside the dividend: an enacted Virginia residential relief payment, shown so the modeled figure can be read against something real. Virginia only; enters no calculation.
- **`carve-out`** — The share of revenue diverted from local dividends to a national floor before anything is distributed locally.
- **`liheap`** — The existing federal energy assistance benefit, shown beside the modeled dividend for scale.
- **`liheap-gap`** — What it would cost for that program to reach every eligible household. Comparison only.
- **`liheap-reach`** — The share of eligible households it actually reaches. Comparison only.

### What households already pay toward data centers

_Context, not inputs. Neither figure enters any calculation; both record who pays for the grid a data center connects to._

- **`network-upgrade-carveout`** — Not used in any calculation. It records what an approved large-load tariff assigns to the customer, and the one category it leaves in ordinary rates.
- **`aes-network-upgrades`** — Not used in any calculation. It is one intervenor's figure for how much of a single project's network upgrades would reach residential bills.
